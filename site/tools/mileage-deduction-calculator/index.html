How the standard mileage rate works

Instead of tracking gas, repairs and insurance, you can deduct a set amount per business mile. For 2026 the rate is 72.5 cents for miles driven January 1 through June 30 and 76 cents for miles driven July 1 through December 31. Split your miles by date, since each period has its own rate.

  • Commuting doesn't count. Business miles are trips between work locations, to clients, or for business errands, not the drive from home to your regular workplace.
  • Keep a mileage log. Record the date, destination, purpose and miles for each trip, as it happens. A log is what the IRS asks for.
  • Parking and tolls for business trips can be deducted in addition to the standard rate.
  • Choosing the method. If you want to use the standard rate for a car you own, you generally have to use it in the first year the car is used for business. Ask us before you choose.
