Content creator tax questions

Yes. Money from ads, subs, bits, tips, memberships, sponsors and affiliate links is taxable income, whether or not you get a 1099. If you create content to make a profit, you report it as a business on Schedule C and pay self-employment tax on the profit.

It depends on whether you run it to make a profit. The IRS looks at factors such as keeping complete books, the time you put in, changing what you do to earn more, and whether you have made a profit in some years. No single factor decides it. Hobby income is still reported, but hobby costs generally can't be deducted.

Only if streaming is a business you run for profit, and only the business-use share. A game you buy to play for fun is a personal cost. If you buy it for content and also play it off stream, keep a record of how you use it.

Usually yes, for the share you use for your content business. Gear that lasts more than a year is generally depreciated, or expensed in one year under Section 179 or bonus depreciation if you qualify. Personal use reduces the deduction.

If a brand gives you a product in exchange for a post, video or review, its fair market value is generally income, the same as being paid in cash. The IRS treats goods received in exchange for services as income in the year you receive them.

15.3% of your net earnings from self-employment: 12.4% for Social Security and 2.9% for Medicare. It usually applies once your net earnings are $400 or more. Half of it is deductible when you work out your income tax.

For 2026, payment platforms send a Form 1099-K when your payments are over $20,000 and you have more than 200 transactions. Sponsors may send a 1099-NEC instead. Income below those amounts is still taxable, so we match every payout to your records.

Most creators do, because platforms and sponsors usually don't withhold tax. Federal estimates for 2026 income are due April 15, June 15 and September 15, 2026 and January 15, 2027. See quarterly estimated taxes.

An LLC can separate your business from your personal finances, but on its own it doesn't lower your tax. Once your profit is steady, an S corp election may cut self-employment tax. See S corp election and LLC formation.
