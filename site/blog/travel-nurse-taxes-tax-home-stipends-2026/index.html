Travel Nurse Taxes: Tax Home, Stipends and What the IRS Checks

Untaxed housing and meal stipends depend on one thing: a real tax home you're traveling away from. Here's the IRS three-factor test, the one-year rule, the FY2026 per diem rates and the records to keep.

By Zain Merchant, ACCANovember 27, 20265 min read
SharePost on LinkedIn

Executive summary

  • Your tax home decides your stipends. Per diem is for lodging and meals while you're away from home on business, so you need a tax home to be away from.
  • The IRS three-factor test: meet all three and your tax home is where you regularly live. Meet only one and you're an itinerant who can't deduct travel.
  • The one-year rule: an assignment expected to last more than 1 year is indefinite, and that city becomes your tax home.
  • FY2026 federal per diem: $178 a day in most of the US ($110 lodging + $68 meals), Oct. 1, 2025 to Sept. 30, 2026.

Bottom line: Keep paying for your main home, go back to it, keep each assignment under a year, and check box 1 and box 12 of every agency W-2.

Travel nurse taxes turn on your tax home. Housing and meal stipends paid under your agency's accountable plan stay out of your taxable wages only when you're traveling away from a real tax home. The IRS uses a three-factor test and a one-year rule to decide. Without a tax home, you're an itinerant and travel isn't deductible.

What is a tax home for travel nurses?

IRS Publication 463 says your tax home is your regular place of business, wherever your family home is.

Travel nurses often have no regular place of business. In that case, your tax home may be the place where you regularly live.

If you have neither, the IRS treats you as an itinerant. Your tax home is wherever you work, and you can't claim travel expenses.

What is the travel nurse three-factor test?

The IRS looks at three factors to decide whether the place you regularly live is your tax home:

  1. You do part of your work in the area of your main home and stay there while working in that area.
  2. You have living costs at your main home that you duplicate because your work takes you away.
  3. You haven't abandoned the area. Family members live at your main home, or you often stay there yourself.
Factors you meetWhat the IRS saysWhat it means for stipends
All threeYour tax home is where you regularly liveYou're away from home on assignment
TwoIt depends on all the factsGray area: get advice
One or noneItinerant; your tax home is wherever you workNo travel deduction; ask your agency how it pays you

A practical example: you pay rent or a mortgage at home, keep your things there, and pick up local shifts between contracts. That usually lines up with all three factors.

Living out of a suitcase with no home costs usually lines up with none.

What is the travel nurse one-year rule?

An assignment realistically expected to last 1 year or less, and that does last 1 year or less, is temporary. Your tax home stays put.

An assignment realistically expected to last more than 1 year is indefinite, even if it ends sooner. That city becomes your tax home.

Extensions matter. A temporary assignment can become indefinite when circumstances change, and a series of short contracts in one place may count as indefinite too.

Are travel nurse stipends taxable?

Under an accountable plan, a per diem allowance at or under the federal rate isn't included in box 1 of your Form W-2.

If the allowance is above the federal rate, the agency reports the amount up to the federal rate in box 12 with code L. The excess goes in box 1 as taxable wages.

An accountable plan has three rules. The expense has a business connection, you account for it within a reasonable time, and you return any excess.

What are the 2026 per diem rates for travel nurses?

Per diem rates follow the federal fiscal year, which runs from Oct. 1 to Sept. 30.

FY2026 rate (Oct. 1, 2025 – Sept. 30, 2026)Per day
Standard lodging, most of the continental US$110
Standard meals and incidentals (M&IE)$68
Standard total$178
High-low method: high-cost locations$319 (incl. $86 M&IE)
High-low method: all other locations$225 (incl. $74 M&IE)

Many cities have their own higher GSA rate. Your agency's plan decides which rate it uses.

Can travel nurses deduct travel expenses?

Usually not, if you're a W-2 employee. The IRS suspended miscellaneous itemized deductions subject to the 2% floor for tax years beginning after 2017. That covers unreimbursed employee travel.

So a nurse on W-2 pay generally can't deduct rent, flights or meals the agency didn't reimburse. The stipend is where the tax benefit comes from.

Keep records that support your tax home for at least 3 years from the date you file: your lease or mortgage, home utility bills, and proof you went back.

Do travel nurses file state tax returns in New Jersey or New York?

Often, yes. New York requires Form IT-203 from nonresidents who received income from New York sources.

New Jersey taxes nonresidents on New Jersey-source income. You figure tax on all your income as if you were a resident, then prorate it by your New Jersey income.

Your home state may also tax all your income and give a credit for tax paid to the work state. We sort that out state by state.
