NJ Sales Tax: How to File the ST-50 Online and Quarterly Due Dates

Every New Jersey business registered to collect sales tax files a quarterly ST-50 return online, even for a quarter with no sales. Here are the due dates, when monthly payments kick in, and how to file through the NJ Tax Portal, including as a guest.

By Zain Merchant, ACCAOctober 23, 20264 min read
SharePost on LinkedIn

Executive summary

  • The return: businesses registered for New Jersey sales tax file the ST-50 quarterly return, even for a quarter with no sales and no tax due.
  • Due dates: the 20th of the month after each quarter ends: January 20, April 20, July 20 and October 20. A weekend or legal holiday moves it to the next business day.
  • Monthly payments: required if you collected more than $30,000 of New Jersey sales and use tax in the prior calendar year and more than $500 in the first or second month of a quarter.
  • How to file: online only, through the NJ Tax Portal, with electronic payment. You can log in or file as a guest.

Bottom line: Put the four ST-50 dates on your calendar, file a zero return when you have no sales, and keep sales tax collected in its own account so the money is there on the 20th.

NJ sales tax filing online is now the only way to file. The Division of Taxation says to file all sales and use tax returns electronically through the New Jersey Tax Portal and to pay electronically. This guide covers who files the ST-50 quarterly return, the due dates, the monthly payment rule and how to file sales tax in NJ with or without a portal login. The NJ sales tax rate is 6.625%.

NJ sales and use tax filing: who files the ST-50 quarterly return

If your business is registered to collect New Jersey sales tax, you file an ST-50 every quarter. The Division is clear that you must file a return for each quarter even if no tax is due or you made no sales. A missed zero return is still a missed return.

Use tax is charged at the same 6.625% rate as sales tax. It applies when your business buys taxable items for its own use without paying New Jersey sales tax, for example from an out-of-state seller that didn't charge it. Businesses registered to collect sales tax report use tax on the ST-50.

ST-50 due dates: when are 3rd quarter sales taxes due?

The ST-50 is due by 11:59 p.m. on the 20th day of the month after the quarter ends. So when are 3rd quarter sales taxes due in New Jersey? The July to September quarter is due October 20.

Monthly payments for larger sellers

You also make monthly payments if both are true:

  • you collected more than $30,000 of New Jersey sales and use tax during the prior calendar year, and
  • you collected more than $500 in the first or second month of the current quarter.

The monthly payment, now filed as a Monthly Voucher in the portal (formerly the ST-51), is due the 20th of the following month. When the amount for a month is $500 or less, you don't submit a voucher, and zero monthly vouchers can't be filed. The quarterly ST-50 still covers the whole quarter.

NJ sales tax filing online, step by step

To file sales tax, NJ businesses use the New Jersey Tax Portal. Here's the process each quarter.

  1. Close the quarter's books. Total your taxable sales, the sales tax you collected and any use tax you owe. Your sales report should tie to the tax you set aside.
  2. Go to the NJ Tax Portal. Log in to your account, or choose File a Return under Quick Access to file without logging in.
  3. Enter the return. Pick sales and use tax as the account type and enter the quarter's figures.
  4. Pay electronically. The Division accepts electronic check, electronic funds transfer and credit card (fees apply).
  5. Save the confirmation number. Your submission is complete when you receive it.

NJ sales tax: file as guest

You don't need a portal account to file. The Division's guest access guide says you may use the portal as a guest to file a tax return or make a payment for a business. As a guest you agree to the terms, enter contact information, then enter your taxpayer ID, business name and account type (the tax you are filing for) before entering the return.
