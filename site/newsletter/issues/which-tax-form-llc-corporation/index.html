What Tax Form Does My LLC or Corporation File? Schedule C, 1065, 1120 or 1120-S

Your business's federal tax form depends on how the IRS classifies it, not on the name on your state paperwork. Here's how to tell whether you file Schedule C, Form 1065, Form 1120-S or Form 1120, when each is due in 2027, and who pays the tax.

Hi there,

One of the most common questions we hear from business owners is which tax return their LLC or corporation actually files. The answer sets your deadline: some returns are due March 15, 2027, a month before your personal return. We've put the four options side by side with their 2027 due dates and who pays the tax.

Executive summary

1
Single-member LLC or sole proprietor: no separate return. The owner reports the business on Schedule C with Form 1040, due April 15, 2027.
2
LLC with two or more members: taxed as a partnership by default and files Form 1065, due March 15, 2027.
3
S corporation (including an LLC that filed Form 2553): files Form 1120-S, due March 15, 2027. Shareholders pay the tax.
4
C corporation (including an LLC that filed Form 8832): files Form 1120, due April 15, 2027, and pays tax at 21%.

Bottom line: Confirm how the IRS classifies your business, calendar that form's due date now, and file the return or an extension on time, even after a loss.

FAQ

“When does an LLC have to file taxes?”

For 2026: March 15, 2027 for a multi-member LLC or an LLC taxed as an S corporation; April 15, 2027 for a single-member LLC or an LLC taxed as a C corporation.
