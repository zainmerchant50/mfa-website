Can I Deduct My Home Internet for Business? 2026 Rules

If you run a business or freelance from home, part of your internet bill can come off your taxes. The catch is the word "part". Here's how to figure your business-use percentage for 2026, what records to keep and who can't claim it at all.

Hi there,

Almost everyone who works from home asks us some version of this: how much of my internet can I deduct for business? The answer depends on whether you're self-employed or an employee, and on the percentage you can back up. We've laid out the rules, a quick way to figure the number and what New Jersey and New York do differently.

Executive summary

1
Self-employed? Yes, the business part. Freelancers, sole proprietors and single-member LLC owners deduct the business-use share of home internet on Schedule C. The personal part isn't deductible.
2
$80 a month at 40% business use = $384 for the year. The percentage has to be one you can back up.
3
W-2 employees generally can't deduct work-from-home costs on the federal return. New York itemizers have a narrow option; New Jersey doesn't allow it.
4
Simplified home office method: $5 per square foot, up to 300 square feet, in place of actual home expenses such as utilities.

Bottom line: Pick a reasonable method, log a few typical weeks now, and keep the bills with your 2026 records.

FAQ

“Can I deduct 100% of my internet if I work from home?”

Only if the connection is used solely for the business, such as a separate business plan. For a shared household connection, deduct the business share you can document.
