Small Business Tax Write-Offs for 2026: What Counts, What's Limited and the Proof to Keep

A practical guide to what you can deduct for 2026, the limits that catch owners out (including two changes this year), and the paperwork that makes each write-off stick.

Hi there,

Year-end is when owners ask, "Can I write that off?" Our new guide covers small business tax write-offs for 2026: what counts, what's limited (meals, mileage, home office, equipment) and the proof the IRS expects you to keep.

Executive summary

1
What counts: ordinary and necessary costs of running your business, and only the business share of anything you also use personally.
2
What's limited: business meals are 50%, entertainment is 0%, client gifts top out at $25 per person, and the simplified home office deduction is capped at $1,500.
3
Two 2026 changes: the business mileage rate rose midyear from 72.5 cents to 76 cents a mile on July 1, and snacks and meals you provide staff at the office are no longer deductible.
4
Proof: every write-off needs a document showing who you paid, how much, when, and what it was for. Keep records at least 3 years (4 for payroll).

Bottom line: Most write-offs are lost to missing paperwork or personal use, not to obscure rules. Check any expense in our Is It Deductible? tool before Dec. 31.

FAQ

“What can I write off as a small business in 2026?”

Ordinary and necessary business costs such as rent, software, supplies, contractors, advertising, insurance, the business share of your phone and car, a qualifying home office, 50% of business meals, and equipment through depreciation, Section 179 or the $2,500 small-purchase rule.
