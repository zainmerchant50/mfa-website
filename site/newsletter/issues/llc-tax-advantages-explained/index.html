What Is an LLC and What Are Its Tax Advantages? (2026)

An LLC is a legal structure, not a tax status. Here's how a one-owner and a multi-owner LLC are taxed by default, what the real tax advantages are, when an S corp election changes the math, and the New Jersey and New York fees to plan for.

Hi there,

Many owners form an LLC expecting a lower tax bill, then find their 2026 return looks the same. We've put the real LLC tax advantages on one page: how the IRS taxes an LLC by default, what changes with an S corp election, and the New Jersey and New York fees to budget for before 2027.

Executive summary

1
An LLC is a legal structure, not a tax status. By default, a one-owner LLC is taxed like a sole proprietor (Schedule C) and a two-plus-owner LLC like a partnership (Form 1065).
2
No tax cut by default: self-employment tax is 15.3% on 92.35% of profit either way, about $8,478 on $60,000 of profit.
3
The real advantage is choice: an LLC can elect to be taxed as a corporation or an S corp. For an S corp start on January 1, 2027, file Form 2553 by March 15, 2027.
4
State costs: New Jersey charges $75 for the LLC annual report; New York's IT-204-LL filing fee runs $25 to $4,500; NYC adds a 4% unincorporated business tax.

Bottom line: Form an LLC for the legal structure and flexibility, then decide on the tax status with real profit numbers before March 15, 2027.

FAQ

“Does forming an LLC lower my taxes?”

Not by itself. A default single-member LLC pays the same income and self-employment tax as a sole proprietor. Savings come from the tax status you choose and how you run the business.
