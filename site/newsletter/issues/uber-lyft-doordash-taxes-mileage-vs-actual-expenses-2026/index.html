Uber, Lyft and DoorDash Taxes 2026: Mileage vs Actual Expenses

App drivers can deduct their car two ways. Here's how the 2026 mileage rates work, when actual expenses win, the first-year rule that locks you in, and the log the IRS expects.

Hi there,

If you drive for Uber, Lyft or DoorDash, your car is usually your biggest deduction, and 2026 has two mileage rates. We've laid out how the standard mileage rate compares with actual car expenses, the first-year rule that decides which you can use later, and the mileage log the IRS expects.

Executive summary

1
2026 has two mileage rates: 72.5¢ a mile for business miles from Jan. 1 to June 30, and 76¢ a mile from July 1 to Dec. 31.
2
Actual expenses means the business share of gas, repairs, tires, insurance, registration and depreciation or lease payments, split by miles.
3
The first year decides: to use the standard rate on a car you own, you must choose it in the car's first business year. On a leased car, it's for the whole lease.
4
Tolls and parking for business trips are deducted on top under either method.

Bottom line: Keep a trip-by-trip mileage log and every car receipt for 2026, then run both methods before you file.
