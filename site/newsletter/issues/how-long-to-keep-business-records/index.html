How Long to Keep Business Records for Taxes (2026)

Before you clear out the filing cabinet at year-end, here's how long the IRS, New Jersey and New York expect you to keep receipts, bank statements, payroll files and asset records, and what you can safely shred.

Hi there,

Year-end is when many owners clear out old boxes of receipts. Before anything goes in the shredder, we've put the IRS, New Jersey and New York record-keeping windows on one page, so you keep what you need and nothing more.

Executive summary

1
3 years is the IRS default: keep the records behind a return for three years from the date you file it. A return filed early counts as filed on the due date.
2
Longer in some cases: 4 years for payroll records, 6 years if you left out more than 25% of your gross income, 7 years for a bad debt or worthless securities deduction, and no limit if a return was never filed.
3
Asset records last longer: keep them until the window closes for the year you sell or dispose of the asset.
4
New Jersey uses 4 years to assess most taxes; New York asks for at least 3 years after filing, and 3 years from the due or filing date for sales tax.

Bottom line: Keep at least four years of records if you file in New Jersey, keep payroll and asset files longer, and scan the rest so nothing gets lost.

FAQ

“How long do business records need to be kept?”

Generally three years after you file for federal purposes, four years for payroll records, and longer for asset records or if income was left off a return. In New Jersey, plan on at least four years.
