Paying an overseas contractor:
which form, and is there a 1099?

Two questions decide almost everything. Ask them before the first payment.

1Is the person a US citizen or US resident (even if living abroad)?
Yes, US person
Form W-9

1099-NEC if you paid $2,000+ in 2026. Due Feb. 1, 2027.

No, foreign individual
Form W-8BEN

Keep it on file. Don't send it to the IRS. Generally valid through the end of the 3rd year after signing.

No, foreign company
Form W-8BEN-E

For an agency or firm abroad. Keep it on file with the contract.

2Foreign person: where is the work performed?
Entirely outside the US
Foreign-source income
  • Generally no Form 1099-NEC
  • No 30% withholding
  • No Form 1042-S
  • Still deductible as contract labor
Any days worked in the US
US-source income for those days
  • 30% withholding unless a treaty exempts it
  • Treaty exemption claimed on Form 8233
  • Reported on Form 1042-S
  • Get advice before the trip

Records to keep for every overseas contractor

  1. Signed contract: work, rate, contractor status
  2. Invoices in their or their company's name
  3. Proof of payment: wire, transfer or platform record
  4. W-8BEN / W-8BEN-E (or W-9) dated before payment 1
  5. Where the work was done, stated on the contract or invoice
  6. Separate expense account for overseas contract labor
There's no special tax break for hiring abroad. The saving is the lower cost; the deduction works the same as for a US contractor.
