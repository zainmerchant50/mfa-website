Executive summary

  • Who files: sole proprietors and single-member LLCs that haven't elected to be taxed as a corporation. The business doesn't file its own federal return; the owner attaches Schedule C to Form 1040.
  • When: April 15, 2027 for the 2026 tax year, or October 15, 2027 with Form 4868. The extension is for filing, not paying.
  • Self-employment tax: 15.3% on 92.35% of net profit once net earnings reach $400, on top of income tax.
  • Deductions to track: business mileage (72.5 cents a mile for January to June 2026, 76 cents for July to December), a home office ($5 per square foot up to 300 square feet with the simplified method) and the qualified business income deduction of up to 20%.

Bottom line: Keep business income and expenses in their own account, make quarterly estimated payments, and keep mileage and receipts as you go rather than in April.

Get the official form from irs.gov: Schedule C (Form 1040) and the Schedule C instructions.

So what is the Schedule C tax form? It's the two-page schedule where a self-employed person reports business income and expenses, titled Profit or Loss From Business. The profit on line 31 flows to your Form 1040 (Schedule 1, line 3) and to Schedule SE, where self-employment tax is figured. Below: who needs to file Schedule C, the 2027 dates and the deductions that matter most for small business taxes.

Who needs to file Schedule C

  • Sole proprietors: anyone running a business or side business in their own name, including freelancers and gig workers.
  • Single-member LLCs: the IRS instructions say that if you are the sole member of a domestic LLC, you file Schedule C, unless the LLC elected to be taxed as a corporation (Form 8832 or Form 2553).
  • Statutory employees (box 13 checked on their W-2): they report that income and the related expenses on Schedule C.

An LLC owned by spouses generally files Form 1065, because the qualified joint venture election that lets spouses each file Schedule C isn't available for a business held in an LLC. Our guide to which tax form your LLC or corporation files and our Form 1065 guide cover the other options.

When does an LLC have to file taxes? Schedule C dates

A single-member LLC has no separate federal due date. Single-member LLC taxes are due with the owner's Form 1040.

Self-employment tax on Schedule C profit

Self-employment tax is Social Security and Medicare for people who work for themselves. The rate is 15.3% (12.4% Social Security and 2.9% Medicare), and it generally applies to 92.35% of net earnings. You owe it once net earnings from self-employment reach $400, and you can deduct half of it when figuring adjusted gross income.

Example: $50,000 of Schedule C profit x 92.35% = $46,175, and $46,175 x 15.3% is about $7,065 of self-employment tax, before any income tax. Our 1099 tax calculator runs the full estimate.

Schedule C deductions worth tracking

  • Car and truck (line 9): the 2026 standard mileage rate is 72.5 cents a mile for January 1 to June 30 and 76 cents for July 1 to December 31. Keep a log of business miles. Try our mileage deduction calculator.
  • Business use of home (line 30): with the simplified method, $5 per square foot of space used regularly and exclusively for business, up to 300 square feet ($1,500). A 200-square-foot office is $1,000. Compare both methods with our home office deduction calculator.
  • Contractors: payments to contractors are deductible, and for 2026 payments the Form 1099-NEC threshold is $2,000.
  • Qualified business income deduction: Schedule C profit may qualify for a deduction of up to 20% of net qualified business income, figured on Form 8995 or 8995-A. It lowers income tax, not self-employment tax.

FAQs

What is the Schedule C tax form, and who needs to file it?

Schedule C reports profit or loss from a business you run as a sole proprietor. Sole proprietors, single-member LLC owners without a corporate election, and statutory employees file it with Form 1040.

Does a single-member LLC file its own tax return?

Not for federal income tax. The owner reports the LLC's income and expenses on Schedule C, unless the LLC elected to be taxed as a corporation.

When is Schedule C due in 2027?

April 15, 2027 for the 2026 tax year, with your Form 1040. Form 4868 moves the filing date to October 15, 2027, but any tax is still due April 15.

Can my spouse and I each file Schedule C for our LLC?

Generally no. A business owned by spouses through an LLC doesn't qualify for the qualified joint venture election, so it usually files Form 1065.
