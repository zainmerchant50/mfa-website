Executive summary

  • Most Airbnb hosts report on Schedule E. If you give guests substantial services, like regular cleaning during a stay, changing linen or maid service, the income goes on Schedule C and may owe 15.3% self-employment tax.
  • The 14-day rule: if the place is also your home and you rent it fewer than 15 days, you don't report the rent at all.
  • Personal-use limit: stay more than the greater of 14 days or 10% of the nights rented at a fair price, and the rental can't show a loss.
  • NJ and NY: marketplace bookings in New Jersey carry 6.625% sales tax and a 5% State Occupancy Fee; New York requires booking services to collect sales tax.

Bottom line: Count your 2026 rented and personal nights before December 31, then pick the schedule from the services you actually provide.

Airbnb host taxes come down to two questions: what you provide and how often you stay there. Space only, with cleaning between guests, usually goes on Schedule E. Hotel-style services during stays move the income to Schedule C. If the place is also your home, the 14-day rule and the personal-use limit decide what you report and deduct.

Is Airbnb income reported on Schedule C or Schedule E?

For the Airbnb Schedule C or Schedule E question, the answer is usually Schedule E. IRS Topic 415 says rental income and expenses are generally reported on Schedule E (Form 1040).

The exception is services. IRS Publication 527 says that if you provide substantial services mainly for your guests' convenience, such as regular cleaning, changing linen or maid service, you report the income and expenses on Schedule C. You may also owe self-employment tax on Schedule SE.

Heat and light, trash collection and cleaning of common areas don't count as substantial services. Decide from what you actually provide, not from what the platform calls you.

What is the 14-day rule for Airbnb?

If you use a dwelling as a home and rent it for fewer than 15 days in the year, you don't report any of the rental income. You also don't deduct any expenses as rental expenses. That's straight from IRS Topic 415.

The rule only helps if the place is also your home. A property you never stay in doesn't get it, so even a few paid nights are reported.

How many personal days can an Airbnb host use?

The IRS treats a rental as your home when your personal-use days are more than the greater of 14 days or 10% of the days you rent it at a fair price. Rent it 200 nights and your limit is 20 personal days.

Airbnb personal use days include days used by you, anyone with an interest in the property, family, and anyone paying less than a fair rent. A day you spend working substantially full time on repairs and maintenance isn't a personal day, even if family uses the place that day.

Go over the limit and you still deduct the rental share of expenses, but not more than your rental income. The rental can't show a loss. Some unused expenses carry forward to the next year.

What can Airbnb hosts deduct?

Airbnb host tax deductions follow the usual rental rules. If you also use the place yourself, divide expenses between rental and personal use by the number of days used for each. Rented 120 nights and stayed 30? About 80% of shared costs is the rental share.

  • Cleaning, supplies and linens for guests.
  • Platform service fees withheld from your payouts.
  • Utilities, insurance, repairs and the rental share of mortgage interest and property tax.
  • Depreciation on the building over 27.5 years, straight line, mid-month convention (Pub 527). Land isn't depreciated.

What is the short-term rental 7-day rule?

IRS Publication 925 says an activity isn't a rental activity if the average guest stay is 7 days or less. The same applies at 30 days or less if you provide significant personal services.

That matters for losses. The $25,000 special allowance for rental real estate phases out between $100,000 and $150,000 of modified AGI, and it's for rental real estate activities, so it generally doesn't help when the activity isn't a rental activity. Whether a short-term rental loss can offset your wages then depends on material participation. Two of the tests: more than 500 hours a year, or more than 100 hours and at least as much as anyone else.

Airbnb taxes in NJ and NY

  • New Jersey: rentals booked through a transient space marketplace carry 6.625% sales tax and a 5% State Occupancy Fee, plus any municipal occupancy tax. The marketplace collects and remits them. Since August 9, 2019, a rental booked directly from the owner isn't subject to sales tax unless it's a professionally managed unit.
  • New York: TB-ST-331 says operators and booking services must collect sales tax on short-term rental occupancy when the rate is more than $2 a day. A booking service that facilitates your rental collects it. An owner renting directly for 3 days or less in a year doesn't.
  • New York City: operators and booking services also charge a $1.50 per unit per day fee.

When are 2026 estimated taxes due for hosts?

Nobody withholds tax from your payouts. The last 2026 estimate is due January 15, 2027 with Form 1040-ES. You can skip it if you file your 2026 return by February 1, 2027 and pay the full balance.

FAQs

Do I pay self-employment tax on Airbnb income?

Usually not on Schedule E. If you provide substantial services and report on Schedule C, self-employment tax of 15.3% may apply once net earnings reach $400.

Does cleaning between guests make it Schedule C?

Not by itself. The IRS points to services during stays, like regular cleaning, changing linen or maid service, mainly for the guest's convenience.

Do I report Airbnb income without a 1099-K?

Yes. Platforms send a 1099-K over $20,000 and 200 transactions, but the income is taxable either way.

Does a day spent fixing up the rental count as personal use?

No, if you work substantially full time on repairs and maintenance that day.
