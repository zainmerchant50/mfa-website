Executive summary

  • Single-member LLC or sole proprietor: no separate return. The owner reports the business on Schedule C with Form 1040, due April 15, 2027.
  • LLC with two or more members: taxed as a partnership by default and files Form 1065, due March 15, 2027.
  • S corporation (including an LLC that filed Form 2553): files Form 1120-S, due March 15, 2027. Shareholders pay the tax.
  • C corporation (including an LLC that filed Form 8832): files Form 1120, due April 15, 2027, and pays tax at 21%.

Bottom line: Confirm how the IRS classifies your business, put that form's due date on your calendar now, and file the return or an extension on time, even after a loss.

How to file taxes for an LLC or corporation starts with one fact: your state paperwork says LLC or corporation, but the IRS decides which federal return you file. So what tax form does a corporation file, and what about an LLC? It depends on the number of owners and any election on Form 8832 or Form 2553. Here are the four options, with 2027 due dates for calendar-year 2026 returns.

Federal filing status for an LLC: the default rules

Without an election, an LLC's classification is:

  • One member: disregarded as separate from its owner for income tax, with no return of its own. An individual owner reports it on the Schedule C tax form (or Schedule E or F) with Form 1040.
  • Two or more members: a partnership filing Form 1065. See our Form 1065 guide for LLCs.

So the usual single-member LLC tax form is Schedule C, plus Schedule SE if net self-employment earnings are $400 or more. For employment taxes, though, the LLC is treated as separate, so it runs any payroll under its own name and EIN.

What tax form does a corporation file?

A corporation files Form 1120 unless it elected S status, whether or not it has taxable income. It pays 21% on taxable income, and shareholders are taxed again on dividends, which the IRS calls a double tax.

A corporation with an S election (no more than 100 shareholders, allowable shareholders only, one class of stock) files Form 1120-S. It generally pays no federal income tax; profit passes to shareholders on Schedules K-1. Our Form 1120-S guide covers distributions and owner pay.

Changing it: an LLC taxed as an S corp or C corp

  • Form 2553 (S corporation): an LLC taxed as an S corp files Form 2553 only, not Form 8832. For S status from January 1, 2027, file during 2026 or by March 15, 2027.
  • Form 8832 (C corporation): it can take effect up to 75 days before or 12 months after filing. After a change, an LLC generally can't change again for 60 months.

An S election adds payroll and a separate return, so run the numbers first with our S corp tax savings calculator.

New Jersey returns

Which NJ LLC tax return you file depends on how the business is taxed. A multi-member LLC with New Jersey income or NJ resident partners files the NJ-1065, and an S corporation files the CBT-100S, both due April 15, 2027. A C corporation files the CBT-100. FAQs

When does an LLC have to file taxes?

For 2026: March 15, 2027 for a multi-member LLC or an LLC taxed as an S corporation; April 15, 2027 for a single-member LLC or an LLC taxed as a C corporation.

Does an LLC with no income have to file?

A multi-member LLC can skip Form 1065 only if it had no income and no deductible expenses or credits. A C corporation files Form 1120 even with no taxable income, and an S corporation files Form 1120-S every year its election is in effect.

What is the Form 1120 due date in 2027?

April 15, 2027 for calendar-year 2026, or October 15, 2027 with Form 7004. Tax is still due April 15.

My spouse and I own an LLC together. Do we file Schedule C?

Generally no, it files Form 1065. The qualified joint venture election that lets spouses each file Schedule C isn't available for a business held in an LLC.

What's the penalty for filing Form 1065 or 1120-S late?

$260 per partner or shareholder per month or part of a month, up to 12 months, for returns due in 2027, even with no tax owed.
