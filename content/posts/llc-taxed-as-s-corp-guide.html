Executive summary

  • How: an LLC gets S corporation tax treatment by filing Form 2553. You don't need to form a new company.
  • Deadline for 2027: file by March 15, 2027 to start January 1, 2027. A calendar-year company can also file at any time during 2026.
  • Who qualifies: 100 or fewer shareholders, all individuals, certain trusts or estates, no nonresident alien shareholders, and one class of stock.
  • What changes: owners who work in the business are paid reasonable wages through payroll, and the company files Form 1120-S plus a state return each year.

Bottom line: The election can lower self-employment tax once profit is comfortably above a reasonable salary. It also adds payroll and filing costs, so run the numbers before you file.

An LLC is not a tax classification. By default, a single-member LLC is taxed as a sole proprietorship and a multi-member LLC as a partnership. An LLC taxed as an S corp keeps its LLC legal structure but is taxed under the S corporation rules. The company generally pays no federal income tax itself. Profit passes to the owners, who report it on their own returns.

Who can have an LLC taxed as an S corp?

The IRS requires the company to be a domestic corporation, or an eligible entity such as an LLC that chooses to be taxed as one. It must also meet these tests:

  • No more than 100 shareholders.
  • Only allowable shareholders: individuals, certain trusts and estates. Partnerships, corporations and nonresident aliens can't be shareholders.
  • One class of stock. Distributions must follow ownership percentages.

When an LLC files Form 2553 and meets these tests, the IRS treats it as having chosen to be taxed as a corporation, so a separate Form 8832 is generally not needed.

Form 2553 deadline for 2027

File Form 2553 no more than 2 months and 15 days after the start of the tax year the election is to take effect, or at any time during the tax year before it. For a calendar-year LLC that wants S status starting January 1, 2027, that means:

  • Any time in 2026, or
  • By March 15, 2027, at the latest.

All shareholders must consent. Miss the date and the election generally starts the following year, unless you qualify for late-election relief under Rev. Proc. 2013-30. That relief requires, among other things, reasonable cause and that the company acted as an S corporation, so don't plan around it.

Reasonable compensation: the rule that decides the savings

An S corporation must pay a shareholder-employee reasonable compensation for the services they perform before non-wage distributions are made. The IRS can reclassify distributions as wages when pay is too low. Reasonable depends on the work, training, experience, time and what similar businesses pay for similar work.

A rough example

Say an LLC has $120,000 of net profit and the owner's reasonable salary is $60,000. As a sole proprietor, self-employment tax is about $16,956 (15.3% of 92.35% of $120,000). As an S corp, payroll tax on the $60,000 salary is $9,180 (15.3%). The difference is about $7,800.

That figure is before the added costs: payroll processing, a Form 1120-S return, a state return and often higher bookkeeping fees. It also ignores income tax effects. If profit is close to a reasonable salary, there is little to save, so run your own numbers with our S corp tax savings calculator.

New Jersey and New York

  • New Jersey: there is no separate state S election for periods beginning on or after December 22, 2022. The federal election carries over. File the CBT-100S with Schedule SJC and proof of federal S status. The minimum tax runs from $375 to $1,500 based on New Jersey gross receipts.
  • New York: a federal S corporation must file Form CT-6 and get approval before filing the CT-3-S. The fixed dollar minimum tax runs from $25 to $4,500 based on New York receipts.

Other states differ, and a few tax S corporations differently, so check your state before you file.

FAQs

Do I need to form a new company to be taxed as an S corp?

No. An existing LLC can file Form 2553 and keep its legal structure.

What is the Form 2553 deadline for 2027?

March 15, 2027 for an election effective January 1, 2027. A calendar-year company can also file at any time during 2026.

Can I file Form 2553 late?

Possibly. Rev. Proc. 2013-30 offers relief when there is reasonable cause and the company has acted as an S corporation, but the conditions are strict.

Do I have to run payroll for myself?

Yes. A shareholder who works in the business must be paid reasonable wages before non-wage distributions.

Is the S election worth it?

It depends on profit, salary, state taxes and the added costs. It tends to make sense when profit is well above a reasonable salary.
