Executive summary

  • Barbers, hairstylists, cosmetologists, nail techs, lash and brow techs and makeup artists are on the IRS list of tipped jobs (codes 603, 605, 606 and 607).
  • The deduction is up to $25,000 a year for 2025 through 2028. It shrinks by $100 for each $1,000 of modified AGI over $150,000 ($300,000 joint).
  • Booth renters can claim it too, but only up to the net profit of the business where the tips were earned.
  • It cuts federal income tax only. Social Security, Medicare and self-employment tax still apply, and it doesn't change your New Jersey return.

Bottom line: Keep a dated tip log and make sure every tip shows up on a W-2, a 1099 or Form 4137, or you can't deduct it.

What counts as a qualified tip?

A qualified tip is a voluntary cash or charged tip from a customer, including tips shared with you. The customer must choose to pay it, and it can't be negotiated.

Cash equivalents count: checks, credit and debit cards, gift cards and app payments in dollars. A mandatory service charge doesn't count unless the customer can change or skip it.

The tip must also be reported to you on a Form W-2, 1099-NEC, 1099-MISC or 1099-K, or by you on Form 4137. Cash tips nobody reports can't be deducted.

Is no tax on tips for self-employed booth renters?

Yes. The IRS says self-employed workers qualify if their occupation is on the list and they meet the other rules. Tips go on Schedule C as business income.

Two limits apply. First, the deduction can't exceed your net income from the business where you earned the tips, figured before this deduction. Second, tips earned in a "specified service trade or business" under the qualified business income rules don't qualify.

For now, IRS transition relief treats both employees and self-employed people in a listed occupation as not working in a specified service business. That relief runs until January 1 of the first calendar year after final regulations are issued, so check the IRS tips page before you plan 2027.

Self-employed people don't follow the employee tip-reporting rules. You still have to be able to prove your tips, so keep a log.

Does it lower Social Security, self-employment or state tax?

No. It's a federal income tax deduction only. Tips are still subject to Social Security and Medicare tax, and booth renters still pay self-employment tax on them.

  • New Jersey: the Division of Taxation says the new federal deductions don't affect the New Jersey income tax return, because New Jersey tax isn't figured from federal adjusted gross income.
  • New York: we couldn't confirm the state treatment on tax.ny.gov as of this date. Ask us before you count on a New York break.

How do you claim it?

Use Schedule 1-A, Part II, attached to Form 1040, 1040-SR or 1040-NR. The total carries to Form 1040, line 13b. You can claim it whether you itemize or take the standard deduction.

You need a Social Security number valid for employment. If you're married, you must file jointly. Employees report cash tips to the employer by the 10th of the next month, unless they're under $20 for the month.

FAQs

Do nail techs qualify for no tax on tips?

Yes. Manicurists and pedicurists are code 605 on the IRS list, so voluntary tips they receive and report can qualify, up to $25,000 a year.

Can I deduct cash tips I never reported?

No. Tips must appear on a W-2, a 1099 or Form 4137. Report all your tips; they're taxable income either way.

Is a booth renter's tips deduction limited?

Yes. It can't exceed the net income, before this deduction, from the business where you earned the tips.

Does no tax on tips cut self-employment tax?

No. The deduction doesn't apply for Social Security, Medicare or self-employment tax.

How long does no tax on tips last?

It covers tax years 2025 through 2028. No deduction is allowed for tax years beginning after December 31, 2028.
