Executive summary

  • 2026 has two mileage rates: 72.5¢ a mile for business miles from Jan. 1 to June 30, and 76¢ a mile from July 1 to Dec. 31.
  • Actual expenses means the business share of gas, repairs, tires, insurance, registration and depreciation or lease payments, split by miles.
  • The first year decides: to use the standard rate on a car you own, you must choose it in the car's first business year. On a leased car, it's for the whole lease.
  • Tolls and parking for business trips are deducted on top under either method.

Bottom line: Keep a trip-by-trip mileage log and every car receipt for 2026, then run both methods before you file.

For Uber, Lyft and DoorDash taxes, you can deduct your car with the standard mileage rate or with actual expenses. For 2026 the standard rate is 72.5¢ a mile through June 30 and 76¢ a mile from July 1. Actual expenses win more often for costly cars with fewer miles. Either way, you need a mileage log.

What is the standard mileage rate for 2026 for rideshare drivers?

The IRS set two business rates for 2026. It raised the rate in the middle of the year (Announcement 2026-11, effective July 1, 2026).

Business miles drivenRate per mile
Jan. 1 – June 30, 202672.5¢

For comparison: all of 202570¢

Because of the split, your log has to show when each mile was driven. A single yearly total isn't enough to apply the right rate.

The rate already covers gas, oil, repairs, tires, insurance and depreciation. You don't deduct those separately.

Can DoorDash drivers deduct actual car expenses instead?

Yes. With actual expenses, you add up what the car really cost and deduct the business share.

The IRS list includes gas, oil, repairs, tires, insurance, registration fees, licenses, and depreciation or lease payments.

The business share comes from your miles. If 80% of the miles you drove were for the apps, you deduct 80% of the costs.

Driving between your home and a regular place of work is commuting, which isn't deductible. Your log should show what each trip was for.

Can I switch from actual expenses to standard mileage?

Only if you started with the standard rate. To use it on a car you own, you must choose it in the first year the car is used in your business. After that, you can pick either method each year.

If you start with actual expenses, the standard rate is off the table for that car. On a leased car, choosing the standard rate means using it for the entire lease, including renewals.

You also can't use the standard rate if you run five or more cars at the same time, or if the car was depreciated with Section 179, bonus depreciation or any method other than straight-line.

Are tolls and parking deductible for rideshare drivers?

Yes. Parking fees and tolls for business trips are deducted separately under either method. Keep the receipts or the toll account statements.

What mileage log do Uber drivers need?

The IRS lists what proves a car expense. For each business use, write down:

  • the date
  • where you drove
  • the business purpose
  • the miles, from start and stop readings

You also need your total miles for the year, so note your odometer on Dec. 31. Keep these records for at least 3 years from the date you file the return.

Do Uber drivers get a 1099-K?

It depends on how you're paid. A third-party payment platform only has to file a 1099-K when your payments top $20,000 and 200 transactions. Card payments have no threshold, and some states set lower limits.

Either way, all of it is taxable. The IRS says income is taxable even if you don't get a Form 1099-K. Use your app's yearly tax summary and your bank deposits.

FAQs

What is the 2026 mileage rate for DoorDash and Uber drivers?

72.5¢ a mile for business miles from Jan. 1 to June 30, 2026, and 76¢ a mile from July 1 to Dec. 31, 2026.

Can I deduct gas if I use the standard mileage rate?

No. Gas, oil, repairs, tires, insurance and depreciation are built into the rate. Tolls and parking for business trips are still deducted on top.

Can I use the standard rate on a leased car?

Yes, but if you choose it, you must use it for the entire lease period, including renewals.

Is my drive from home to my first pickup deductible?

It depends on your facts. Commuting between home and a regular place of work isn't deductible, so log the purpose of every trip and ask us how your driving pattern is treated.

Do I have to report app income if I didn't get a 1099?

Yes. All income is taxable unless the law says otherwise, even without a Form 1099-K.
