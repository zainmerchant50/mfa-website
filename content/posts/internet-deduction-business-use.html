Executive summary

  • Self-employed? Yes, the business part. Freelancers, sole proprietors and single-member LLC owners deduct the business-use share of home internet on Schedule C. The personal part isn't deductible.
  • $80 a month at 40% business use = $384 for the year. The percentage has to be one you can back up.
  • W-2 employees generally can't deduct work-from-home costs on the federal return. New York itemizers have a narrow option; New Jersey doesn't allow it.
  • Simplified home office method: $5 per square foot, up to 300 square feet, in place of actual home expenses such as utilities.

Bottom line: Pick a reasonable method, log a few typical weeks now, and keep the bills with your 2026 records.

Can I deduct my home internet for business? If you work for yourself, yes: you can deduct the share of your internet bill that serves the business. The IRS rule is simple. When an expense is partly business and partly personal, you separate the two, and the personal part is generally not deductible. The work is in figuring, and proving, the split.

Home internet tax deduction for the self-employed

To be deductible, a business expense has to be ordinary (common in your field) and necessary (helpful and appropriate for your business). For most freelancers, consultants and online sellers, internet clears that bar easily.

You claim it on Schedule C with your other business costs. If you add a separate internet plan that only the business uses, there's no personal part to split out, so the whole bill is a business expense. The IRS applies the same idea to phones: a second line used only for business is deductible, while the base rate of the first phone line into your home is not.

How much of my internet can I deduct for business?

Your internet business use percentage. The IRS doesn't publish a set formula for internet, so use a method that's reasonable and that you can document. Common approaches:

  1. Time: business hours online as a share of total household hours online.
  2. Devices or users: if one of three heavy users is the business, a share close to that.
  3. A usage log: track a few typical weeks and apply the result to the year.

Internet deduction and the simplified home office method

If you have a qualifying home office, you choose between two methods. The regular method (Form 8829) deducts the business share of actual home costs, such as rent or mortgage interest, insurance and utilities. The simplified method allows $5 per square foot of the area used regularly and exclusively for business, up to 300 square feet, and you can't also deduct those actual home expenses.

Under either method, business expenses that aren't about the use of the home itself, such as supplies and certain telephone costs, are still deducted on their own Schedule C lines. The IRS doesn't say in its home office guidance whether home internet counts as a home utility or as one of those separate business expenses. If you use the simplified method and also claim internet, settle the treatment with your preparer and apply it the same way every year. Our home office deduction calculator compares both methods.

Can W-2 employees deduct internet?

Generally not on the federal return. The IRS says miscellaneous itemized deductions subject to the 2% floor were eliminated for tax years beginning after 2017, so most employees can't deduct unreimbursed work expenses. A few groups still can, including Armed Forces reservists, qualified performing artists and fee-basis state or local government officials. If your employer requires home internet, ask whether it will reimburse you.

Internet deduction: New Jersey and New York

  • New Jersey: business profits on the NJ-1040 start from your federal Schedule C, so a properly figured internet deduction generally carries over. New Jersey does not allow employee business expenses.
  • New York: the self-employed claim internet on the federal Schedule C as above. New York residents who itemize can list unreimbursed employee expenses on Form IT-196, but only the part of those job and miscellaneous expenses above 2% of the income on line 19 of the IT-201 or IT-203 counts.

FAQs

Can I deduct 100% of my internet if I work from home?

Only if the connection is used solely for the business, such as a separate business plan. For a shared household connection, deduct the business share you can document.

Do I need a home office to deduct internet?

No. The home office deduction has its own tests, but internet used for your business is a business expense on its own. A home office simply adds other deductions.

What records should I keep for the internet deduction?

The monthly bills or a year-end statement, a short usage log from typical weeks, and a note on how you figured the percentage.

Can I deduct my internet as a W-2 employee in New York?

Possibly on the state return, if you itemize on Form IT-196 and your total job and miscellaneous expenses pass the 2% threshold. Not on the federal return.
