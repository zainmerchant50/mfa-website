Executive summary

  • $400 is the number. You must file a federal return if your net earnings from self-employment are $400 or more, even if you owe no income tax.
  • The $2,000 rule is for payers. Businesses send a Form 1099-NEC for 2026 pay of $2,000 or more. You report all income, with or without a form.
  • Self-employment tax is 15.3% on 92.35% of net earnings. $10,000 of profit means about $1,413, on top of income tax.
  • Next date: Jan. 15, 2027, the fourth 2026 estimated payment, if you expect to owe $1,000 or more.

Bottom line: If your side income cleared a few hundred dollars of profit in 2026, plan to file, and set money aside for the tax now.

So, how much do you have to make on a 1099 to file taxes? For most freelancers, gig workers and side hustlers, the answer is $400 of net earnings from self-employment. That's profit after business expenses, not what clients paid you. Here's how the rule works for 2026 income.

The $400 self-employment rule

The IRS rule: you have to file a return if your net earnings from self-employment were $400 or more. That includes side, part-time and temporary work.

Net earnings are figured on Schedule SE as 92.35% of your profit from Schedule C. In practice, the line falls at about $434 of profit after expenses. Below that, you generally don't owe self-employment tax, though you may still need to file for other reasons (below).

This is different from the general filing thresholds. For 2025 returns, a single filer under 65 with only wage income doesn't need to file below $15,750 of gross income. Self-employment income doesn't get that cushion. The 2026 standard deduction rises to $16,100 (single) and $32,200 (married filing jointly), but the $400 rule doesn't change.

Do I have to report 1099 income under $2,000?

Yes. For payments made in 2026, a business sends you a Form 1099-NEC only if it paid you $2,000 or more. Payment apps and marketplaces send a Form 1099-K only when payments exceed $20,000 in more than 200 transactions. Those thresholds tell the payer when to send a form. They don't change what you report.

The IRS says you must report all income on your return, even if you don't receive Forms 1099. So if three clients each paid you $1,500, you may get no forms at all, and you still report $4,500 of income.

Self-employment tax on 1099 income

Self-employment tax is Social Security and Medicare for people who work for themselves. The rate is 15.3% (12.4% Social Security plus 2.9% Medicare) on 92.35% of net earnings. You can deduct half of it when you figure your adjusted gross income, and you owe it even if you already get Social Security or Medicare benefits.

Estimated taxes for 1099 income

No one withholds tax from 1099 pay, so you pay during the year. You generally need estimated payments if you expect to owe $1,000 or more after withholding and credits. You can usually avoid the underpayment penalty by paying the smaller of 90% of this year's tax or 100% of last year's (110% if last year's AGI was over $150,000).

The fourth 2026 payment is due Jan. 15, 2027. You can skip it if you file your 2026 return and pay the full balance by Feb. 1, 2027. Have a W-2 job too? Raising your paycheck withholding can cover the tax instead.

1099 income: New Jersey and New York filing

  • New Jersey: you file an NJ-1040 if gross income from everywhere is more than $10,000 (single or married filing separately) or $20,000 (married filing jointly, head of household or qualifying widow(er)). Net business profits count. NJ estimated payments are required if you expect to owe more than $400, with the fourth 2026 payment due Jan. 15, 2027.
  • New York: residents file if they must file a federal return, or if federal AGI plus New York additions is over $4,000 ($3,100 for a single filer who can be claimed as a dependent). New York City and Yonkers residents may have added filing responsibilities. You can skip NY estimated payments only if you expect to owe less than $300 each of New York State, New York City and Yonkers tax.

FAQs

I made $300 on a 1099. Do I have to file?

Not because of self-employment tax, since $300 is under the $400 threshold. You may still need to file if your total income is over the regular filing threshold, and filing can make sense to get back withholding or claim refundable credits.

Is the $400 based on what clients paid me or on my profit?

On profit. Net earnings are figured after business expenses, then multiplied by 92.35% on Schedule SE.

I didn't get a 1099. Do I still report the income?

Yes. The IRS says all income is reportable whether or not you receive a Form 1099 or 1099-K.

Do I owe self-employment tax if I also have a W-2 job?

Yes, on your self-employment profit. If your wages and self-employment earnings together pass the 2026 Social Security wage base of $184,500, the Social Security part stops at that cap. Medicare still applies to all of it.
