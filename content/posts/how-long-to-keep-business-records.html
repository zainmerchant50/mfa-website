Executive summary

  • 3 years is the IRS default: keep the records behind a return for three years from the date you file it. A return filed early counts as filed on the due date.
  • Longer in some cases: 4 years for payroll records, 6 years if you left out more than 25% of your gross income, 7 years for a bad debt or worthless securities deduction, and no limit if a return was never filed.
  • Asset records last longer: keep them until the window closes for the year you sell or dispose of the asset.
  • New Jersey uses 4 years to assess most taxes; New York asks for at least 3 years after filing, and 3 years from the due or filing date for sales tax.

Bottom line: Keep at least four years of records if you file in New Jersey, keep payroll and asset files longer, and scan the rest so nothing gets lost.

How long to keep business records comes down to one idea: keep anything that supports a tax return until the government can no longer question that return. For most small businesses that's three years for the IRS, but payroll files, asset records and New Jersey returns need longer. Here's the full list, so you know what you can shred at year-end and what stays.

How long to keep business records: the IRS rules

When people ask how long to keep tax records, the IRS answer is tied to the period of limitations: the time it has to assess more tax, or you have to claim a refund. Each period runs from the date you filed the return.

SituationKeep records for
Most returns (no exception below)3 years
You claim a refund after filing3 years from filing or 2 years from paying, whichever is later
Income left off the return over 25% of the gross income shown6 years
Bad debt or worthless securities deduction7 years
No return filed, or a fraudulent returnIndefinitely

Example: a 2025 return filed on April 15, 2026 is generally open to IRS changes for three years from that date, into April 2029. Keep the bank statements, invoices and receipts behind it at least that long.

How long to keep payroll records

If you have employees, keep employment tax records for at least four years after the tax is due or paid, whichever is later. That includes your payroll reports, payroll tax returns and tax deposit records. IRS Publication 15 lists the full set.

Asset records: keep them until you sell

Keep the purchase invoice, closing statement and depreciation records for equipment, vehicles and property until the period of limitations ends for the year you sell or dispose of the asset. You need them to figure depreciation and your gain or loss. A machine bought in 2020 and sold in 2028 means keeping its records into the 2030s.

How long to keep receipts for taxes

The IRS says to keep receipts, canceled checks and other documents that support the income, deductions and credits on your return. IRS Publication 583 lists the usual ones: cash register tapes, deposit slips, invoices, Forms 1099-NEC and 1099-MISC, account statements, credit card slips and petty cash slips. How long to keep tax records in NJ and New York

  • New Jersey: the Division of Taxation generally has four years from the date you filed to assess more tax, one year longer than the IRS. If you file in New Jersey, four years is the safer minimum for your business records.
  • New York: the Tax Department says to keep records and supporting documents for at least three years after you file. For NY sales tax record keeping, vendors must keep sales and purchase records for at least three years from the return's due date or filing date, whichever is later, and longer if the department asks, for example during an audit.

Can I keep digital copies of receipts?

Yes. The IRS applies the same rules to electronic records as to paper, and you may destroy the paper originals once your electronic storage system has been tested and meets the requirements in Revenue Procedure 97-22. In practice that means clear, complete scans you can find and print. New York lets sales tax vendors keep electronic records too, as long as they can be provided in a readable electronic form.

FAQs

How long do business records need to be kept?

Generally three years after you file for federal purposes, four years for payroll records, and longer for asset records or if income was left off a return. In New Jersey, plan on at least four years.

Should I keep copies of my filed tax returns?

Yes. The IRS recommends keeping copies of filed returns, because they help you prepare future returns and amend past ones. We suggest keeping them permanently.

Can I throw away records once the IRS window closes?

Check first whether a lender, insurer or other party needs them longer. The IRS notes that other parties can require records beyond the tax period.
