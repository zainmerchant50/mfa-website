02 · Estimates2026 Small Business Tax Starter Kit · 03

Quarterly estimated taxes, in one page

No employer is withholding tax from business profit, so the IRS expects you to pay as you go, four times a year.

$1,000
Federal trigger
Estimates are generally required if you expect to owe $1,000 or more for 2026 after withholding and credits.
$400 / $300
NJ / NY trigger
New Jersey: if you expect to owe more than $400. New York: $300 or more of state, NYC or Yonkers tax.
15.3%
Self-employment tax
On 92.35% of net profit, on top of income tax. The Social Security part stops at $184,500 for 2026.

Avoid the underpayment penalty: the safe harbors

For 2026 you generally owe no federal underpayment penalty if your withholding and on-time estimates cover the smaller of:

  • 90% of your 2026 tax, or
  • 100% of your 2025 tax (110% if your 2025 adjusted gross income was over $150,000, or $75,000 if married filing separately).
Quick example. $60,000 of 2026 net profit means about $8,478 of self-employment tax ($60,000 × 92.35% × 15.3%) before any income tax. Half of the self-employment tax is deductible on your Form 1040. Run your own numbers with the quarterly estimated tax calculator.

03 · Deductions2026 Small Business Tax Starter Kit · 04

Deduction checklist for 2026

Tick what applies to you, then make sure your books show it. Each item needs a receipt or record: date, amount, who and the business purpose.

  • Home officeSimplified method: $5 per sq ft, up to 300 sq ft ($1,500 max). The space must be used only and regularly for work.
  • Business mileage72.5¢ a mile Jan. to June 2026, 76¢ July to Dec. Keep a log. Commuting never counts.
  • Parking and tollsOn business trips, on top of mileage.
  • Phone and internetThe business-use share of the bill.
  • Software and subscriptionsQuickBooks, Canva, Zoom and the like; business share only.
  • Small equipmentItems of $2,500 or less each (or per invoice) can be written off at once if you elect the de minimis safe harbor.
  • Larger equipmentSection 179 up to $2,560,000 for 2026, or 100% bonus depreciation for property acquired after Jan. 19, 2025.
  • Business meals50% with a client or contact, with a real business purpose.
  • Travel away overnightFlights, hotels and rides in full; meals on the trip at 50%.
  • ContractorsIn full. Send a 1099-NEC at $2,000 or more for 2026 payments.
  • Self-employed health insurancePremiums for you and your family, if the business made a profit.
  • Retirement savingsSEP-IRA: the lesser of 25% of pay or $72,000 for 2026. Solo 401(k): $24,500 deferral, plus $8,000 at 50 or older.
  • Half of self-employment taxDeducted on your Form 1040; it lowers income tax.
  • Startup costsUp to $5,000 in your first year, phased out above $50,000 of costs; the rest over 180 months.
  • Bank and card processing feesStripe, Square and PayPal fees on business sales.
  • Accounting, legal and tax-prep feesBusiness share in full.
  • Advertising and marketingAds, website, business cards.
  • Client giftsUp to $25 per person per year.
  • Business insuranceLiability, property, professional coverage.
  • Courses and trainingWhen they keep up skills for the work you already do.

Common traps: not deductible

  • CommutingHome to your regular workplace.
  • EntertainmentGame tickets, golf, shows, even with a client.
  • Fines and penaltiesParking tickets, IRS penalties.
  • Club and gym duesSocial, athletic and fitness clubs.
  • Snacks and meals for staff at workNew for 2026: no longer deductible, with narrow exceptions.

1099 readiness checklist

For payments made in 2026, filed in early 2027. The big change: the threshold for most 1099s went from $600 to $2,000.

$2,000
1099-NEC / 1099-MISC
Services, rent, medical and other income paid in 2026 ($600 for 2025 payments).
Feb 1
1099-NEC deadline
To contractors and the IRS, Monday, February 1, 2027.
$20,000
1099-K (processors)
Plus more than 200 transactions. Card and app payments you make are on the processor's form.

Work through it in this order

  • List everyone you paid for services in 2026Freelancers, contractors, attorneys, landlords, repair and cleaning services.
  • Take out card and payment-app paymentsPaid by credit card, PayPal Goods & Services, Stripe or Square? The processor reports those on Form 1099-K, not you.
  • Check each payee's Form W-9Individuals, sole proprietors and LLCs taxed as partnerships generally get a 1099. Corporations generally don't, except attorneys and medical providers.
  • Collect missing W-9s nowBefore January, when people are slow to answer. Use a secure upload, not plain email, since the form has a tax ID.
  • Total what you paid each payeeFlag anyone at $2,000 or more for services, rent or other income; $10 or more in royalties; $600 or more in gross proceeds to an attorney.
  • Check names and tax IDs matchA mismatch can bring IRS notices and backup withholding.
  • Choose how you'll fileThe IRS IRIS portal or a provider such as QuickBooks. E-filing is required if you file 10 or more information returns in total.
  • Send 1099-NECs by Feb 1, 2027To each payee and to the IRS. There is no automatic extension.
  • Send 1099-MISCs on timePayee copies by Feb 1, 2027 (Feb. 16, 2027 for box 8 or 10); IRS by Mar 1 on paper or Mar 31, 2027 electronically.
  • Check your stateSome states, including New Jersey, need their own copy or annual reconciliation.
  • Withheld tax because there was no tax ID?Then you file the 1099 whatever the amount.

05 · Bookkeeping2026 Small Business Tax Starter Kit · 06

A 10-step monthly close

Clean monthly books are what make tax season short. Pick the same day each month, for example the 10th, and work down the list.

  1. Download every statementEach bank, credit card, loan and payment app (PayPal, Stripe, Square, Venmo business) for the month.
  2. Categorize every transactionNothing left in "uncategorized" or "ask my accountant". Split mixed charges.
  3. Reconcile each accountYour books should match each statement's ending balance to the cent. Investigate any difference now, not in March.
  4. Match receiptsAttach or file receipts and note the business purpose for meals, travel and anything unusual.
  5. Separate personal from businessRecord owner draws and contributions; move personal charges out of business expenses.
  6. Review who owes youFollow up on invoices more than 30 days old.
  7. Review what you oweSchedule bills; check nothing was paid twice.
  8. Record the non-obviousPayroll, loan payments split into principal and interest, processor fees netted from deposits.
  9. Read your P&L and balance sheetCompare with last month and last year. Ask why anything moved a lot.
  10. Set aside taxMove your estimate share to a separate tax savings account and note the next due date (page 3).
At year-end, add: count inventory, list equipment bought during the year with dates and costs, total your mileage log, and confirm every contractor's W-9 (page 5).

LLC vs S corp: when it's worth a look

An S corporation is a tax election, not a different kind of company. An LLC can keep its default tax treatment or elect S corp status on Form 2553.

Default LLC (sole owner)

Profit goes on Schedule C. All of it is subject to self-employment tax of 15.3% on 92.35% of net profit (the Social Security part up to $184,500 for 2026), plus income tax.

LLC taxed as an S corp

You pay yourself a reasonable salary through payroll, and Social Security and Medicare apply to that salary. The rest of the profit passes to you on a K-1 without self-employment tax. You file Form 1120-S by March 15, 2027.

An illustration, not a recommendation

Default LLCS corp election
Net profit before owner pay$100,000$100,000
Owner salary through payrollNone$60,000
Self-employment or payroll tax$14,130 (SE tax)$9,180 (employee + employer shares)
Difference before added costsabout $4,950 a year, before payroll service fees, the 1120-S return, state taxes and fees, and income tax effects

Worth a look when

  • Profit is steady and well above a reasonable salary for your role
  • You're ready to run payroll every month, on time
  • Your books are clean and business money has its own account
  • You expect the business to keep earning at this level

Probably not yet when

  • Profit is small, uneven or still unknown
  • You'd pay yourself an unrealistically low salary to make it work
  • Payroll filings and a separate return feel like a burden
  • You live or work in a state where the election adds tax or fees
Timing: to be taxed as an S corp for all of 2027, a calendar-year business generally files Form 2553 by March 15, 2027. New Jersey and New York have their own S corp rules, so check both before electing. Try the S corp vs LLC savings estimator with your numbers.
